Training-Free Expert Pruning

Expert pruning shrinks an already-trained mixture-of-experts model without retraining, distillation or gradients—but deleting an expert re-routes the tokens it served, so scoring experts in isolation measures a quantity the model never uses. We make the counterfactual cheap to observe: one all-expert calibration capture turns every layer-local leave-one-out deletion into a cache-based replay, and a single deletion-response signature per expert drives the selection. We prune LingBot-Video 30B-A3B at a 50% budget with no training: a pruned replica fits a single 48 GB card while generation quality remains essentially intact under the official scorer.

Expert pruning is a re-routing problem, not a subtraction problem. (a) Deleting an expert changes the grouped top-k decision: the tokens it served are re-routed. (b) One instrumented all-expert capture makes every layer-local deletion replayable at the captured states, so the whole 6,144-deletion study costs no further forward passes. (c) At a 50% budget this yields a smaller checkpoint and a rise in the official VBench Total, without any training.

Abstract

Video diffusion transformers (DiTs) increasingly rely on mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures, where only a sparse subset of a large expert bank is activated per token. While dynamic sparsity reduces active compute, it leaves the full expert storage footprint intact. Furthermore, conventional one-shot pruning criteria rely on static activation or routing statistics, failing to capture the layer-level re-routing behavior triggered after expert deletion. To make this post-deletion behavior observable without prohibitive cost, we record all expert outputs and router states across matched conditional and unconditional tokens in a single all-expert calibration pass. Consequently, replaying any single-expert deletion and its grouped re-routing reduces to tensor arithmetic on cached states, requiring zero additional model forward passes. Using these deletion responses, we formulate retained-expert selection as an Overall Diversity Loss (ODL): each pruned expert is matched with its nearest retained expert and the summed nearest-neighbor cosine distances define a set-level coverage loss under which deleted experts closer to retained ones cost less, directly prioritizing functional coverage. We solve this optimization in two stages: an intra-layer local search combining greedy initialization, single-swap refinement, and simulated annealing to select retained set under a fixed retention ratio, and an inter-layer regression-guided search that optimizes expert-count allocation across layers. Evaluated on LingBot-Video 30B-A3B, pruning 50% of the experts (6,144 → 3,072) without fine-tuning reduces the checkpoint footprint from 57 GB to 30 GB, enabling single-card deployment on a 48 GB GPU while generation quality remains essentially intact: we observe the official VBench Total rise from 0.7941 to 0.8115 across a fixed 284-case protocol. Across all other tested retention budgets, DIET consistently outperforms competitive baselines adapted from large language models.

Method

  • One instrumented capture. A single pass over 120 calibration cases—evaluating all 128 experts per layer on matched conditional and unconditional tokens—records every expert output and every router score (368,640 matched token pairs), once.
  • Frozen-state replay. Deleting an expert and recomputing the grouped top-k routing at the captured states reproduces the layer-local leave-one-out deletion with zero additional forward passes: all 6,144 deletions become cache arithmetic.
  • Deletion-response signatures. Each expert is summarised by the change it causes when removed. Signatures are used strictly for their directional orientation: concurrent removals violate linear superposition, so absolute perturbation size does not reliably reflect joint dynamics, while direction measures functional interchangeability.
  • Overall diversity loss selection. The retained set is chosen to minimise an overall diversity loss (ODL)—each deleted expert pays the distance to its nearest retained expert in deletion-response space—under the grouped-router feasibility constraints, preserving diverse deletion responses rather than merely active experts.
  • Regression-guided budget. Per-layer kept counts come from an offline search on a 142-case budget-search set that linearises the official VBench Total; the 284-case protocol's 142 strictly held-out prompts are evaluated separately.

Analysis

What breaks under joint deletions is additivity, not distance. Joint multi-expert removals violate linear superposition: the cosine similarity between a joint deletion perturbation and the sum of its single-deletion responses falls from 1.00 at k = 1 to 0.66 at k = 64, with reconstruction error rising to 0.95. Objectives built on summed responses degrade by 5.44 points, consistent with the non-additivity measured here.

Pruning is not routing preservation. The best-scoring mask is the most disruptive to the original routing, while the criteria that lose the most quality preserve routing the most—so minimising routing change is the wrong objective.
